Why these questions?

Most presentations inform. Very few change how we see, think, or act.

We've all sat through talks that were informative, polished — and forgotten by the time we reached the exit.

The Five Diagnostic Questions are a way of looking more closely at what makes communication genuinely valuable.

And they aren't just for evaluating other people's presentations. Use them to question, shape, and strengthen your own talks, pitches, and presentations.

The five questions

Five questions.
One framework.

Each question opens a different dimension of what makes professional communication work — and gives you a different way of looking at a speech, presentation, or pitch.

Question 01

The Reframe

Does this show me a familiar problem in an unfamiliar way?

The diagnostic signal
  • A shift in perspective, not just new information
  • The moment you see something old with new eyes
Question 02

The Lens

Is expertise serving the argument — or just on display?

The diagnostic signal
  • Knowledge that illuminates, not impresses
  • Authority that supports the idea, not the ego
Question 03

The Takeaway

Can I name ONE idea I'm carrying with me?

The diagnostic signal
  • A crystallised insight — not a list of points
  • Something you can repeat in a single sentence
Question 04

The Invitation

Am I thinking with the speaker — or being told what to think?

The diagnostic signal
  • Gaps left for your own judgement
  • The feeling of being respected, not lectured
Question 05

The Value Added

Am I different — clearer, more capable — than I was before?

The diagnostic signal
  • A genuine shift in how you see, think, or act
  • Transformation, not just information
